CASE 01 2024 Air Canada Tribunal ruled against them

A grieving man asked
a chatbot about a
bereavement refund.

It gave him the wrong policy — in a calm, official, confident voice. He trusted it. He booked a full-price ticket.

"While a chatbot has an interactive component, it is still just a part of Air Canada's website."
— BC Civil Resolution Tribunal, Moffatt v. Air Canada
The pattern → RAG
A retrieval miss doesn't throw an error. It produces a fluent, confident, wrong answer. Without a score guardrail, a hit and a miss look identical.
Source: Moffatt v. Air Canada (BC CRT, 2024) · BBC · The Guardian02

How a confident
wrong answer happens

01
The question arrives. Ordinary request, ordinary user.
02
Retrieval misses. Similar words, different meaning.
03
The model uses it anyway. It cannot see that retrieval failed.
04
Confident, fluent, wrong. No error. No "I don't know."
The rule
A retrieval miss is worse than no retrieval at all — because a miss looks exactly like success.

CASE 02 2026 Amazon · internal 860% over budget
$1.8M

One Claude job. Five months
before anyone noticed.

It was meant to match author records to product listings. It ran 860% over budget — and failed anyway. Two more projects overshot by $541K and $134K.

Buggy code crashes. A misconfigured model job just keeps running — and quietly bills you.
— the difference that turns a mistake into a seven-figure invoice
The pattern → Cost & Latency Budgets
Set a per-request and per-user ceiling. Profile the dominant stage. Cache, route, reduce — in that order. Never reach for the frontier model by default.
Source: Financial Times / Tom's Hardware (Jul 2026)04
